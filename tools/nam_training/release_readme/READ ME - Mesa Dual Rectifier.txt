GROUND RULES FOR A GOOD TONE
(These apply to any NAM capture and any NAM player, not only to AMPSURD.)

1. Input level matters as much as the guitar.
   A capture reacts to how loud your guitar arrives, just like a real amp: too quiet sounds thin and
   weak, with less gain and less punch. Set the input gain of your audio interface as high as possible
   without clipping - play your hardest and leave a little headroom (the meter should not reach red).
   If your player has an input level / calibration setting, set it to match your interface.

2. Use a speaker cabinet IR.
   These are captures of the amplifier only, without the speaker cabinet. Without a cabinet impulse
   response (IR) the sound is harsh and fizzy. Load a cab IR after the capture.

3. Your guitar's volume knob works like on the real amp.
   Rolling it back cleans the sound up. Pickups matter too: hotter pickups give more gain and
   compression, single coils less gain and more clarity (and more hum).

4. Keep noise out at the source.
   High gain amplifies everything, including hum from single coils, cables, power supplies and screens.
   Turn away from the computer screen, use good cables, and use a noise gate for high gain.

5. Work at 48 kHz with a small buffer.
   The captures were made at 48 kHz; other sample rates work but are converted. A small buffer
   (64-128 samples) keeps the delay low so the amp feels natural to play.

6. Less gain is often more.
   A tight boost pedal in front with lower amp gain often sounds clearer and heavier in a mix than
   maximum gain. Shape the sound with the amp's tone controls first, and use EQ after the cab only
   to fit the mix.
